# Contact force frames and friction API audit

Read-only audit of the saved diff_left_plus_0.10_20261020 case and the installed Newton/MJWarp + IsaacLab Newton source. This closes the force-frame question left open in contact_probe_analysis.md; it does not identify the steering-loss cause. No simulator, GPU, training, or source/model/protocol changes were made.

## Finding

The force and friction fields used by this probe are consistent with world-frame force in newtons. The MJWarp adapter rotates its contact-frame wrench to world coordinates before Newton SensorContact projects the top three force components against the world contact normal. The adapter also flips the sign so the stored force is on Newton shape0, matching the Newton contact API; the sensor flips sign when aggregating onto shape1. The saved friction vectors therefore do not show a proven frame or sign bug.

The old probe did not save live per-contact mj_contact_friction, mj_contact_dim/condim, mj_model.opt.cone, or contact solreffriction. It saved configured shape_mu=0.8, aggregate force vectors, and contact identities/normals. Thus its vectors can be checked for frame and energy-consistent direction, but cannot alone certify the runtime contact coefficient or explain why available grip did not produce body yaw. The active solver config leaves cone at MJWarpSolverCfg's "pyramidal" default, but that value was not captured from the live mj_model.opt.cone in this old run.

## Source chain and field meaning

- Installed Newton sim/contacts.py:421–425 documents Contacts.force as a spatial wrench in world frame: top three are force [N]; bottom three are torque [N·m] about body0 COM. rigid_contact_force at :293–295 is marked unused.
- Installed MuJoCo Warp support.py:368–392 says contact_force_fn extracts a contact-frame wrench and, when to_world_frame=True, rotates both force and moment by the contact-frame matrix. Newton solvers/mujoco/kernels.py:1493–1498, 1520–1534 takes the world contact normal and stores the negated wrench because MuJoCo Warp returns force on geom2 while Newton stores force on shape0. There is no moment-arm shift in that adapter.
- Installed Newton sensors/sensor_contact.py:98–118 reads only spatial_top(Contacts.force), normalizes contact_normal, removes dot(force, normal)*normal as friction, and aggregates world vectors with opposite signs for the two shapes. Newton SensorContact output documentation (:378–394, :684–699) describes these aggregates as [N] world-frame forces. IsaacLab Newton copies those arrays in contact_sensor.py:381–417, 471–502; its data/API docs give net_forces_w, net_normal_forces_w, and net_friction_forces_w in world coordinates and newtons (contact_sensor_data.py:83–96, 143–154, 208–220; shared base docs base_contact_sensor_data.py:29–37).
- The Newton ContactSensor force matrices are per configured filter-counterpart aggregate, not per contact point/pair: the destination kernel copies one vector for (env, sensor, filter) (contact_sensor_kernels.py:19–29, 59–65), and the data shape is (N, S, F) (contact_sensor_data.py:112–119). They are None unless filter expressions are configured (contact_sensor.py:307, 381–388). This probe configured no filter matrix (contact_transfer_probe.py:389–396), so its per-wheel vectors sum all current counterparts/manifold contacts on that sensing body. The upstream IsaacLab Newton test marks the filtered-matrix case skipped for use_mujoco_contacts=False because normal-matrix reporting is nondeterministic across hardware (test_contact_sensor.py:470–477). A matrix would still not expose individual manifold-point forces.
- Separate API defect, irrelevant to the sampled friction vectors: the Newton API promises Contacts.force's lower three components are torque about body0 COM, but the MJWarp adapter only rotates the contact wrench and stores it directly; it does not add/subtract the contact lever-arm cross force. This can make Contacts.force.bottom semantically wrong for off-COM contacts. It does not affect IsaacLab's current ContactSensor, which reads only the top three force components; do not alter the vendor source to address this task.

## Friction settings and what was actually recorded

isaac_field/env.py:73–75 configures use_mujoco_contacts=False and NewtonShapeCfg(..., mu=.8); MJWarpSolverCfg declares cone="pyramidal" by default (external/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab_newton/isaaclab_newton/physics/mjwarp_manager_cfg.py:90). These are configuration facts, not values serialized from the old live contact records. Newton's MJWarp contact builder copies each shape's slide/torsion/rolling coefficients into geom_friction (newton/solvers/mujoco/kernels.py:2481–2485) and combines geom friction and condim to form each generated contact (:120–181 and :553–665); without live mj_contact_friction and mj_contact_dim, the old raw does not independently confirm the effective pair fields.

The installed solver code contains solreffriction plumbing. For the active Newton-generated-contact conversion, the geometry contact_params() starts it at (0,0) (newton/solvers/mujoco/kernels.py:181); a conditional positive shape-material kf mapping may overwrite it (:632–644). MuJoCo Warp constraint code selects a nonzero contact solreffriction for non-normal directions (:4270–4274, 4691–4695). The use_mujoco_contacts=False conversion kernel does not take pair_solreffriction as an input, so a native MuJoCo pair override is not evidence of a value on this Newton-contact path. The old probe neither captured the generated contact array nor proves a custom kf mapping was active. Treat installed support as present, but current-run effective solreffriction as unverified.

## Offline falsification check on saved probe

Using the saved policy-rate records only, I estimated flat-bottom contact slip as:

    v_slip ≈ v_root + omega_root × (wheel_center − root_position − 0.045 m·world_Z) − qd·0.045 m·body_forward

This assumes a 45 mm wheel radius, flat contact at the bottom of each wheel, and body-forward as the rolling direction; it is a sign/frame diagnostic, not exact contact-point kinematics. In the 160 drive samples, per-wheel mean longitudinal slip was about −0.384 m/s left and +0.366 m/s right, while mean lateral slip was near zero (+0.00016, +0.00017 m/s). Mean longitudinal friction was +9.37 N left and −9.29 N right; mean lateral friction was only −1.74 N and +1.49 N. On samples with |F_friction|>2 N and |v_slip|>0.05 m/s, F_friction·v_slip was non-positive in 100% of samples on both sides; mean cosine to slip was −0.775 left and −0.773 right. This supports the expected aggregate friction sign and overall dissipation. It does not verify individual contact forces or rule out pyramidal-cone effects at individual points.

For the final second, aggregate forward friction projected by side was +61.767 N left and −56.945 N right. Total upward normal force averaged 293.111 N, near the 30 kg weight, but support was not evenly continuous by wheel. These are per-wheel body aggregates, not calibrated tire/track traction measurements. The yaw result remains weak (−0.07255 rad/s last-second mean against the nominal −2.03252 rad/s), so neither "friction is too small" nor "solver/API is at fault" follows from this check.

## Minimal next check

For the already-preparing one-case A/B, record the actual post-build mj_model.opt.cone, generated per-contact friction, dim, and solreffriction arrays alongside shape0/shape1, world normal/contact position, efc_address/efc_force, and Contacts.force for the same solver step. Reconstruct exact wheel contact-point velocity from root/body linear and angular velocity plus wheel spin, then check per-contact tangential-force direction and F_t·v_slip≤0 (within numerical tolerance). This can falsify a force-frame/sign mismatch and distinguish longitudinal drive friction from lateral constraint response without a retrain. Use the aggregate/filter matrix only as a cross-check, not as per-contact truth; the old contact-matrix test has the nondeterminism caveat above.
